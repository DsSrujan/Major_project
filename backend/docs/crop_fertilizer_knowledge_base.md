# Crop Fertilizer Knowledge Base (24 Crops)

**Source document:** Kerala Agricultural University (KAU), *Package of Practices Recommendations: Crops 2016*, 15th edition (file: pop2016.pdf). Page numbers below are the book's printed page numbers.

**Conventions used in this file**
- All values are copied from the source. Nothing was added from outside knowledge. Where the source is silent, the field says "Not specified in source".
- Nutrient doses: N = nitrogen, P2O5 = phosphorus, K2O = potash. Where the source writes only "phosphorus" or "potash", it is stated as printed.
- "Nutrient Targets" are the KAU PoP recommendations, not ICAR/STCR values.
- Tree and plantation crops are recommended per plant (g or kg per palm/plant/year) in the source. Per-hectare equivalents are shown only where the source gives plant density, and are marked **(derived)**.
- This document is Kerala-specific. Climate, season and soil-region names (Kuttanad, Onattukara, Kole, Palakkad etc.) refer to Kerala.
- The source gives **no numeric rainfall thresholds** (such as ">15 mm in 48 hours") for holding back fertilizer. Timing and weather notes below contain only what the source actually says.
- **Not in the source:** Pearl Millet / Bajra has no crop section in this document, so it is not included.

# Crop: Rice / Paddy (Oryza sativa)

- **Soil & Climate Requirements:**
  - Soil Type: Comes up well in different soil types. Special regions: Kuttanad (including Kari soils), Kole lands, Onattukara (sandy loam with iron toxicity), Pokkali, uplands (modan), Wayanad.
  - Optimal pH: 5.0 to 8.0 is suitable for normal growth.
  - Climate: Critical mean temperature for flowering and fertilization is 16 to 20 °C; during ripening 18 to 32 °C. Temperature above 35 °C affects grain filling.

- **Nutrient Targets (KAU PoP 2016), kg/ha (Table 4):**

| Kind of land / region | Variety | N | P2O5 | K2O |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Uplands (modan) | Upland local varieties | 40 | 20 | 30 |
| Uplands (modan) | High yielding short duration | 60 | 30 | 30 |
| Wetlands (all regions) | High yielding short duration | 70 | 35 | 35 |
| Wetlands (all regions) | High yielding medium duration | 90 | 45 | 45 |
| Wetlands (all regions) | Local varieties | 40 | 20 | 20 |
| Wetlands (all regions) | H4 | 70 | 45 | 45 |
| Wetlands (all regions) | Mahsuri | 50 | 25 | 25 |
| Kole lands* | Short duration varieties | 90 | 35 | 45 |
| Kole lands* | Medium duration high yielding | 110 | 45 | 45 |
| Kattukampal and Ponnani kole lands* | Medium duration high yielding | 110 | 45 | 55 |
| Wetland (Kuttanad)** | Medium duration high yielding | 90 | 45 | 15 |
| Onattukkara | Dhanya | 60 | 30 | 30 |
| Koottumundakan | Photo-insensitive varieties, first crop | 40 | 20 | 20 |
| Koottumundakan | Photosensitive varieties, second crop | 20 | 10 | 10 |

  - \* Location-specific recommendations. Strict surveillance of pests and diseases is a must.
  - \*\* Applies wherever soil K status is medium to high on soil test, and where straw incorporation is practised and tidal contribution of the nutrient is significant. Phosphorus can be skipped for six seasons without significant yield reduction in riverine alluvium soils medium to high in available P.
  - Koottumundakan system (separate recommendation): 20:10:10 kg/ha for first crop and 30:15:15 kg/ha for second crop. Northern region economic dose: 40:20:20 kg/ha for virippu and 20:10:10 kg/ha for photosensitive mundakan.

- **Fertilizer Prescription & Application Protocol:**
  - Organic manure: FYM, compost or green leaf at 5 t/ha, incorporated while ploughing. Vermicompost or coirpith compost at 2.5 t/ha can replace 5 t/ha FYM in Onattukara. Biofertilizers are recommended.
  - Fertilizer materials mentioned: urea (N), rock phosphate or superphosphate (P), zinc sulphate, dolomite, magnesium sulphate, magnesite, ammonium phosphate sulphate.
  - Application method: Apply fertilizers on drained soil at final ploughing and levelling and mix thoroughly. For top dressing, where water is assured, drain the field one day before application and re-flood after twelve hours.
  - Timing / splits (Table 6 summary):
    - Upland and direct-seeded wetland: N in three equal splits (basal, tillering about 3 weeks after seeding, panicle initiation about 30 days before flowering). Full P as basal. K either all basal, or half basal and half at panicle initiation.
    - Wetland transplanted, high yielding short duration: N 2/3 basal and 1/3 at 5 to 7 days before panicle initiation. Full P basal. K half basal, half at panicle initiation.
    - Wetland transplanted, high yielding medium duration: N half basal, half at 5 to 7 days before panicle initiation. Full P basal. K half basal, half at panicle initiation.
    - Mahsuri (transplanted): N half basal, 1/4 at 40 days after planting, 1/4 at 60 days after planting.
    - Onattukara (general): N half basal, 1/4 at tillering, 1/4 at panicle initiation. In very coarse soils, N and K may be given in five equal splits.
    - Onattukara (coarse sandy loam with high percolation): N and K in five equal splits at planting, 15th, 38th, 53rd and 70th day for medium duration varieties.
    - Light soils or soils with high leaching: apply N in three or four splits depending on variety duration.
    - Koottumundakan: second crop fertilizer as a single dose immediately after harvest of the first crop.
  - Weather Guardrail / Timing Warning:
    - In the first crop season, if basal N is not possible because of incessant rains, the basal dose can be shifted to 15 days after transplanting.
    - Basal N may be postponed to initial tillering, especially in the rainy season (Thiruvananthapuram and Malappuram districts).
    - In Kari soils avoid cracking by prolonged drying (leads to severe acidity).
    - Under drought or waterlogging, N can be applied as a foliar spray: urea at 15% concentration with power sprayer or 5% with high-volume sprayer, limited to 15 kg/ha per application.

- **Additional Useful Info:**
  - Seasons (general): Virippu (April-May to Sept-Oct), Mundakan (Sept-Oct to Dec-Jan), Puncha (Dec-Jan to Mar-Apr). Other regions have separate calendars in the source (Table 1).
  - Liming: Absolutely necessary when pH is below 5.5; advisable between 5.5 and 6.5. Apply 600 kg/ha in two splits: 350 kg/ha basal at first ploughing and 250 kg/ha about one month after sowing/transplanting. Keep a one-week gap between lime and fertilizer application.
  - Kari soils of Kuttanad: dolomite 450 kg/ha in two splits (half at initial ploughing, half as top dressing one week before the panicle-initiation fertilizer).
  - Zinc deficiency: zinc sulphate 20 kg/ha. Do not apply zinc sulphate and potash on the same day. Effect can last up to five years, so monitor soil zinc before repeating.
  - Magnesium deficient soils: 20 kg MgO/ha as basal (magnesium sulphate 16% MgO, magnesite 40% MgO or dolomite 10% MgO).
  - Sulphur: where soil has less than 10 ppm CaCl2-extractable S or 15 ppm phosphate-extractable S, replace urea with ammonium phosphate sulphate. For medium duration rice in brown hydromorphic soils, ammonium phosphate sulphate can supply 25 kg S/ha (ad hoc).
  - Iron-toxic laterite soils: 120 kg K2O/ha + lime 150 kg/ha + silica 100 kg/ha (as sodium silicate 250 kg/ha, fine silica 100 kg/ha or rice husk ash 500 kg/ha).
  - Onattukara with iron toxicity: 5 t organic matter (or 2.5 t vermicompost) and 67.5 kg K2O/ha.
  - Straw incorporation maintains soil K. If soil analysis then shows low K, apply 15 kg K2O/ha.
  - Sequential cropping: 50% of nutrient need (N equivalent) as organics and 50% as fertilizer in kharif, and full fertilizer in rabi, enhances yield. Organics should go in 3 weeks before transplanting.
  - Urea efficiency: mix urea with six times its weight of slightly moist soil and apply 24 to 28 hours after mixing; or mix with oilcakes (1 part cake to 5 parts urea).
  - Water management: keep about 1.5 cm at transplanting, raise gradually to about 5 cm until maximum tillering, drain 13 days before harvest.
  - Green manuring: cowpea 12.5 kg/ha with dry seeded rice, or daincha 20 kg/ha with wet seeded rice (seed rate 60 kg/ha).
  - Source pages: 1 to 21.

# Crop: Ragi / Finger Millet (Eleusine coracana)

- **Soil & Climate Requirements:**
  - Soil Type: Mostly red lateritic soils. Relatively fertile, well drained soils are most suitable.
  - Optimal pH: Not specified in source.
  - Climate: Annual rainfall 700 to 1200 mm. Does not tolerate heavy rainfall and needs a dry spell at grain ripening. Grows well at 1000 to 2000 m altitude with average temperature 27 °C.

- **Nutrient Targets (KAU PoP 2016):**
  - Nitrogen (N): 22.5 kg/ha before sowing/planting + 22.5 kg/ha top dressing (total 45 kg/ha)
  - Phosphorus (P): 22.5 kg/ha (as printed, "phosphorus")
  - Potassium (K): 22.5 kg/ha (as printed, "potash")

- **Fertilizer Prescription & Application Protocol:**
  - Organic manure: FYM or compost 5 t/ha, incorporated after ploughing the field 3 to 4 times. Nursery: FYM/compost 5 t/ha in the nursery beds.
  - Fertilizer materials mentioned: ammonium sulphate (nursery, 1 kg per 100 m2 one week before uprooting seedlings).
  - Application method: Apply N, P and K at 22.5 kg/ha each before sowing or planting.
  - Timing: Top dress N at 22.5 kg/ha 21 days after sowing or planting. Weeding should be done three weeks after sowing and completed before top dressing.
  - Weather Guardrail / Timing Warning: Not specified in source.

- **Additional Useful Info:**
  - Seasons: main June-September; late July-October; summer Dec-Jan to March-April (not season bound if moisture is available).
  - Seed rate: direct sown 5 kg/ha; transplanted 4 to 5 kg/ha. Spacing 25 x 15 cm. Nursery area of 480 m2 per hectare. Transplant at three weeks.
  - Varieties: PR-202, K-2, Co-2, Co-7, Co-8, Co-9, Co-10.
  - Irrigation: weekly irrigation increases growth rate and yield.
  - Harvest when ears are yellowish brown.
  - Source page: 43.

# Crop: Maize / Corn (Zea mays)

- **Soil & Climate Requirements:**
  - Soil Type: Fertile, well-drained soil.
  - Optimal pH: 5.5 to 8.0; optimum 6.0 to 7.0.
  - Climate: Can be grown throughout the year from sea level to about 300 m altitude. Grows best with rainfall of 600 to 900 mm.

- **Nutrient Targets (KAU PoP 2016):**
  - Nitrogen (N): 135 kg/ha
  - Phosphorus (P): 65 kg/ha (as printed, "phosphorus")
  - Potassium (K): 15 kg/ha (as printed, "potash")

- **Fertilizer Prescription & Application Protocol:**
  - Organic manure: FYM/compost 25 t/ha at land preparation.
  - Fertilizer materials mentioned: Not specified in source.
  - Application method: Apply full dose of P and K and 1/3 of N as basal.
  - Timing: 1/3 N at 30 to 40 days after sowing and the remaining 1/3 N at 60 to 70 days after sowing.
  - Weather Guardrail / Timing Warning: Not specified in source.

- **Additional Useful Info:**
  - Season: rainfed June-July or August-September; irrigated January-February.
  - Seed rate 20 kg/ha. Spacing 60 x 23 cm for rainy season crop. One seed per hole.
  - Hybrids: Ganga Hybrid-1, Ganga Hybrid-101, Deccan hybrid, Renjit, Hi-Starch. Composites: Kissan Composite, Amber, Vijay, Vikram, Sona, Jawahar.
  - Weeding: hand hoeing on the 21st and 45th day. Irrigation on the day of sowing and the third day, then every 10 to 15 days.
  - Source page: 42.

# Crop: Sorghum / Jowar (Sorghum bicolor)

- **Soil & Climate Requirements:**
  - Soil Type: Fairly tolerant to alkalinity and salinity. Other soil details not specified in source.
  - Optimal pH: Not specified in source.
  - Climate: Hot and warm localities. Optimum temperature for growth 30 °C. Needs about 250 to 400 mm rainfall. Excess moisture and prolonged drought are harmful.

- **Nutrient Targets (KAU PoP 2016), per ha:**

| Nutrient | Irrigated crop | Rainfed crop |
|---|---|---|
| N | 90 kg | 45 kg |
| P2O5 | 45 kg | 25 kg |
| K2O | 45 kg | 25 kg |

- **Fertilizer Prescription & Application Protocol:**
  - Organic manure: FYM/compost 5 t/ha for both irrigated and rainfed crops.
  - Fertilizer materials mentioned: Not specified in source.
  - Application method: Apply FYM and the entire P and K as basal.
  - Timing: N in two equal splits, half as basal and the rest 30 days after sowing.
  - Weather Guardrail / Timing Warning: Not specified in source.

- **Additional Useful Info:**
  - Seasons: rainfed May-August; irrigated January-April.
  - Seed rate 12 to 15 kg/ha, two seeds per hole at 45 x 15 cm.
  - Varieties: Co.1, Co-10, Co-12, Co-17, K-1, K-2; hybrids CSH-1 to CSH-4, Co-11, Co-1.
  - Thinning, weeding and hoeing on the 20th day. Irrigate on day of sowing then at 10-day intervals.
  - Source pages: 42 to 43.

# Crop: Areca Nut (Areca catechu)

- **Soil & Climate Requirements:**
  - Soil Type: Laterites, red loams and alluvial soils. Deep, well drained soil without a high water table.
  - Optimal pH: Not specified in source.
  - Climate: Grows from almost sea level up to 1000 m altitude where rainfall is abundant and well distributed, or under irrigation.

- **Nutrient Targets (KAU PoP 2016):**
  - Adult palms: N:P2O5:K2O = 100:40:140 g/palm/year.
  - Dose schedule by age: 1/3 dose in first year, 2/3 dose in second year, full dose from third year onwards.
  - Approx. per hectare at 2.7 m x 2.7 m spacing (about 1372 palms/ha) **(derived)**: N 137, P2O5 55, K2O 192 kg/ha.
  - Region-specific yellow leaf disease schedules (g/palm/year N:P2O5:K2O, with sulphur and micronutrients):
    - Converted paddy fields: 100:40:200, in two splits (February and September) under irrigation.
    - Garden land: 100:40:250, in two splits (February and September).
    - Terraced upland: 100:40:250, in two splits (February and September).

- **Fertilizer Prescription & Application Protocol:**
  - Organic manure: Green leaf and compost, each 12 kg per palm per year from the first year of planting, applied in September-October. Yellow-leaf areas: minimum 10 kg organic manure per palm per year (15 kg in terraced upland).
  - Fertilizer materials mentioned: For yellow-leaf-affected palms the source names single super phosphate, ammonium sulphate or ammonium phosphate sulphate as sulphur sources (minimum 100 g S/palm/year), plus magnesium sulphate 60 g, borax 20 g and zinc sulphate 20 g per palm per year. Lime is used in acidic soils. Specific N, P and K carriers are not named for adult palms.
  - Application method: Circular basins 15 to 20 cm deep with radius 0.75 to 1.0 m from the palm. Apply the second dose around the base after weeding and mix into soil by light forking.
  - Timing: Irrigated: two splits, first September-October and second February. Rainfed: second dose March-April after receipt of summer rains. In acidic soils, broadcast lime at 0.5 kg per palm once in two or three years and fork in during March-April. In yellow-leaf areas, lime 150 g per palm per year in the basin opened in February.
  - Weather Guardrail / Timing Warning: Rainfed second dose is given only after summer rains are received (March-April). Provide drainage channels (25 to 30 cm deep) between rows and drain out water during heavy rainfall to prevent waterlogging. No numeric rainfall threshold is given.

- **Additional Useful Info:**
  - Planting: May-June in well-drained soils; August-September in clayey soils. Spacing 2.7 x 2.7 m. Pit 60 x 60 x 60 cm. Plant tall quick-growing shade trees on southern and western sides. Banana can be planted between rows for shade up to 4 to 5 years.
  - Irrigation: once in 4 to 7 days depending on soil; about 175 litres per palm per irrigation. West coast schedule: every 7 to 8 days (Nov-Dec), every 6 days (Jan-Feb), every 3 to 5 days (Mar-May).
  - Green manure cover crops: Mimosa invisa, Stylosanthes gracilis, Calopogonium muconoides sown April-May and cut and applied in September-October.
  - Intercrops: elephant foot yam, pineapple, pepper, betel vine, banana, guinea grass, cocoa, ginger, cardamom (manure intercrops separately).
  - Varieties: Mangala, Sumangala, Sreemangala, Mohitnagar, VTLAH 1, VTLAH 2.
  - Nut splitting is linked in the source to hyper-nutrition or a sudden flush of water after drought; borax spray 2 g/litre is effective.
  - Source pages: 152 to 155.

# Crop: Coconut (Cocos nucifera)

- **Soil & Climate Requirements:**
  - Soil Type: Laterite, coastal sandy, alluvial and reclaimed marshy lowland soils. Choose deep (at least 1.5 m), well drained soil. Avoid shallow soils with underlying rock, low-lying areas with water stagnation and heavy clayey soils.
  - Optimal pH: Tolerates pH 5.0 to 8.0 and salinity.
  - Climate: Equatorial climate with high humidity. Ideal mean annual temperature 27 °C with 5 to 7 °C diurnal variation. Well-distributed rainfall of 1300 to 2300 mm per year.

- **Nutrient Targets (KAU PoP 2016), kg/palm/year (Table 24):**

| Situation | N | P2O5 | K2O |
|---|---|---|---|
| General: average management | 0.34 | 0.17 | 0.68 |
| General: good management | 0.50 | 0.32 | 1.20 |
| Reclaimed clayey soils (as in Kuttanad) | 0.25 | 0.35 | 0.90 |
| Red loam soils (southern Kerala) | 0.68 | 0.23 | 0.90 |
| Hybrids and high yielding palms, irrigated | 1.00 | 0.50 | 2.00 |
| Hybrids and high yielding palms, rainfed | 0.50 | 0.32 | 1.20 |

  - Sandy and sandy loams of Onattukara and similar situations, and hybrid palms in root (wilt) affected areas: 500 g N + 300 g P2O5 + 1000 g K2O along with 500 g MgSO4 per palm per year.
  - Palms yielding more than 100 nuts per year: add 10 g N, 5 g P2O5 and 15 g K2O for every nut above 100.
  - Young palms (share of adult dose): 3 months 1/10; 1 year 1/3; 2 years 2/3; 3 years onwards full dose.
  - Approx. per hectare at triangular 7.6 m spacing (198 palms/ha) **(derived)**: average management about 67:34:135 kg N:P2O5:K2O/ha; hybrid irrigated about 198:99:396 kg/ha.

- **Fertilizer Prescription & Application Protocol:**
  - Organic manure: In soils low in organic matter (except reclaimed clayey and alluvial soils) apply 15 to 25 kg organic matter per palm per year during June-July from the second year. Forest leaves, cattle manure, coir dust or coconut shredding at 10 kg per pit in first three years and 15 to 25 kg thereafter help establishment in sandy and coastal soils.
  - Fertilizer materials mentioned: magnesium sulphate (recommended for the whole state), lime or dolomite, sodium chloride (as substitute for K, see below).
  - Application method: Circular basins at 2.0 m radius from the base, 10 cm deep, opened after onset of southwest monsoon. Split doses can be applied with irrigation water in summer.
  - Timing: Rainfed: two splits, 1/3 in April-June (early southwest monsoon showers) and 2/3 in September-October. Irrigated: three or four equal doses in April-May, August-September, December and February-March. Lime or dolomite in April-May, magnesium sulphate in August-September, organic matter in June-July. Adult palm needs 1.0 kg dolomite or 1.0 kg lime + 0.5 kg MgSO4 per year.
  - Weather Guardrail / Timing Warning: Low-lying areas: apply fertilizer in one single dose or two split doses after the water table recedes. No numeric rainfall threshold is given.

- **Additional Useful Info:**
  - Where available P2O5 is above 10 ppm, phosphatic fertilizer can be skipped for a few years until it falls to 10 ppm.
  - In laterite soils, 50% of K2O can be replaced by Na2O as sodium chloride.
  - Spacing: triangular 7.6 m (198 plants/ha); square 7.6 to 9 m (170 to 120); single hedge 5 m x 9 m (220); double hedge 5 x 5 m with 9 m between pairs (280). Lateritic gravelly rainfed north Kerala: closer spacing for 250 palms/ha.
  - Planting: May with pre-monsoon rains (April under assured irrigation; September in low-lying areas). Lateritic soils: 2 kg common salt per pit about six months before planting.
  - Young seedlings: irrigate 45 litres per seedling every 4 days in dry summer for the first two years.
  - Green manure and cover crops: sunhemp, Tephrosia purpurea, Indigofera hirsuta, Pueraria; cover crops Calopogonium, Mimosa invisa, Stylosanthes; sow in April-May and plough in during August-September.
  - Husk burial for moisture conservation lasts about 5 to 7 years.
  - Intercrops: palms aged 8 to 25 years are not suitable for inter or mixed cropping, except ginger and turmeric (shade tolerant) which can be grown at 15 to 25 years.
  - Varieties: West Coast Tall, Chandrasankara, Kerasankara, Anandaganga, Keraganga and others (see source).
  - Source pages: 96 to 102.

# Crop: Oil Palm (Elaeis guineensis)

- **Soil & Climate Requirements:**
  - Soil Type: Wide range of tropical soils. Adult palms withstand occasional waterlogging. Avoid frequently waterlogged, extremely sandy and hard lateritic soils.
  - Optimal pH: Not specified in source.
  - Climate: Mean maximum temperature 30 to 32 °C and at least five hours of sunshine per day. Well-distributed annual rainfall of 200 cm or more; tolerates two to four months of dry spell.

- **Nutrient Targets (KAU PoP 2016), g/palm/year N:P2O5:K2O:**

| Age | N:P2O5:K2O |
|---|---|
| First year | 400:200:400 |
| Second year | 800:400:800 |
| Third year onwards | 1200:600:1200 |

  - Approx. per hectare at 140 palms/ha **(derived)**: third year onwards about 168:84:168 kg N:P2O5:K2O/ha.
  - Nursery seedlings: mixture with 15 g N, 15 g P2O5 and 6 g K2O; apply 8 g in five litres of water for 100 seedlings when seedlings are two months and eight months old.

- **Fertilizer Prescription & Application Protocol:**
  - Organic manure: Green leaf or compost is advantageous, especially where soil is poor in organic matter.
  - Fertilizer materials mentioned: Magnesium only if deficiency symptoms are noticed.
  - Application method: Within 2 m diameter around the palm and forked in.
  - Timing: Two equal split doses, May and September.
  - Weather Guardrail / Timing Warning: Not specified in source.

- **Additional Useful Info:**
  - Only recommended variety: Tenera (hybrid of Dura and Pisifera).
  - Planting: triangular system at 9 m spacing (140 palms/ha), preferably at the onset of monsoon in May-June. Pit 50 x 50 x 50 cm.
  - First harvest 3.5 to 4 years after planting.
  - Pruning: retain about 40 leaves on the crown once yielding.
  - Do not use copper fungicides on seedlings (copper burn).
  - Source pages: 113 to 115.

# Crop: Coffee (Arabica and Robusta) (Coffea spp.)

- **Soil & Climate Requirements:**
  - Soil Type: Deep, friable, rich in organic matter (both species).
  - Optimal pH: 6.0 to 6.5.
  - Climate, Arabica: Elevation 1000 to 1500 m; annual rainfall 1600 to 2500 mm; blossom rain March-April (2.5 to 4.0 cm); backing rain April-May (5 to 7.5 cm); medium to light shade; 15 to 25 °C; RH 70 to 80%.
  - Climate, Robusta: Elevation 500 to 1000 m; annual rainfall 1000 to 2000 mm; blossom rain Feb-March (2.0 to 4.0 cm); backing rain April-May (5 to 7.5 cm); uniform thin shade; 20 to 30 °C; RH 80 to 90%.

- **Nutrient Targets (KAU PoP 2016):**
  - Young plants (Table 29), g N:P:K per plant per year:

| Species and age | N:P:K (g/plant/year) |
|---|---|
| Arabica 1st and 2nd year | 20:10:20 |
| Arabica 3rd and 4th year | 25:15:25 |
| Robusta 1st to 3rd year | 38:28:38 |
| Robusta 4th year | 40:30:40 |

  - Yielding plants (Table 30): dose by yield level. The source text states doses for coffee in kg/ha.

| Yield level (kg) | Suggested N:P:K (kg) |
|---|---|
| 100 | 30:27:30 |
| 200 | 40:34:40 |
| 300 | 50:41:50 |
| 400 | 60:48:60 |
| 500 | 70:55:70 |
| 600 | 80:62:80 |
| 700 | 90:69:90 |
| 800 | 100:76:100 |
| 900 | 110:83:120 |
| 1000 | 120:90:120 |

  - Straight fertilizer quantities (Combination A, with rock phosphate at 16% P2O5) for yield levels 100 / 500 / 1000: urea 65 / 170 / 308 kg; rock phosphate 135 / 275 / 450 kg; MOP 50 / 116 / 200 kg. Other combinations (B and C) are in the source.

- **Fertilizer Prescription & Application Protocol:**
  - Organic manure: 250 g FYM or compost per planting pit in poor soils. Nursery bags: 6 parts jungle soil, 2 parts cattle manure, 1 part sand.
  - Fertilizer materials mentioned: urea, rock phosphate, muriate of potash, DAP, complex fertilizers 17:17:17 and 19:19:19, zinc sulphate (foliar).
  - Application method: Not specified in detail in source beyond the tables.
  - Timing: Apply the suggested dose in a minimum of 2 to 3 splits, or as many splits as possible. Nursery seedlings: manure once in two months with urea in water or supernatant of fermented cowdung.
  - Weather Guardrail / Timing Warning: In addition to the suggested dose, 50 kg urea per acre may be broadcast during a break in the monsoon to combat fruit drop due to soil saturation.

- **Additional Useful Info:**
  - Planting: pits of 45 x 45 x 45 cm opened in April; covered in June. Spacing: tall arabica 2.1 x 2.1 m; semi-dwarf (Cauvery) 1.8 x 1.8 m; dwarf (S 7) 1.5 x 1.5 m; CxR hybrid 2.5 x 2.5 m; robusta 3.0 x 3.0 m. Planting seasons June and September.
  - Shade: about 70% daylight is optimum; dadap as lower canopy in arabica; silver oak planted in June.
  - Dieback management includes correcting soil acidity with lime, thick mulch and foliar nutrients.
  - Recommended varieties: Arabica S.6, Sln.9, Chandragiri (Nelliampathis and Idukki); Robusta S.274 and CxR (Wayanad, Nelliampathis, Idukki). All arabica varieties are discouraged in Wayanad.
  - Source pages: 167 to 173.

# Crop: Tea (Camellia sinensis)

- **Soil & Climate Requirements:**
  - Soil Type: Acidic with good drainage.
  - Optimal pH: Around 5.0.
  - Climate: Maximum temperature 16 to 32 °C; well-distributed rainfall of about 150 cm per year. RH around 80% most of the time and never below 40%. Area should not be frost prone.

- **Nutrient Targets (KAU PoP 2016), young tea, kg/ha/annum:**
  - Soils with pH above 5.5 (use water-soluble P):

| Age | N:P2O5:K2O | No. of splits |
|---|---|---|
| 1st year | 180:60:180 | 5 |
| 2nd year | 240:80:240 | 6 |
| 3rd year onwards up to 1st pruning | 300:100:300 | 6 |

  - Soils with pH between 4.5 and 5.5 (N:K2O, plus 90 kg P/ha every year in one application, as printed):

| Age | N:K2O | No. of splits |
|---|---|---|
| 1st year | 180:270 | 5 |
| 2nd year | 240:360 | 6 |
| 3rd year | 340:450 | 6 |
| 4th year and above | 300:300 | 6 |

  - Soils with pH below 4.5 (N:K2O:MgO, plus 90 kg P/ha every year in one application, as printed):

| Age | N:K2O:MgO | No. of splits |
|---|---|---|
| 1st year | 180:270:30 | 5 |
| 2nd year | 240:360:40 | 6 |
| 3rd year | 300:450:50 | 6 |
| 4th year and above | 300:300:50 | 6 |

  - Fertilizer per bush is calculated assuming a population of 13,000 per ha.
  - Mature tea: the source says the rate varies with yield and soil test values and the N:K2O ratio varies with the stage of pruning, but the printed page gives no table for it.

# General Reference: Fertilizer Use Efficiency and Conversions (all crops)

*Source: KAU PoP 2016, "Guidelines for Maximizing Fertilizer Use Efficiency" (pp. 315 to 317) and Appendices 2 to 4 (pp. 352 to 353).*

- **Nitrogen management**
  - Most crops recover only 25 to 35% of applied N. Losses occur by ammonia volatilisation, denitrification, immobilization, leaching and run-off.
  - Use split application; more splits for long duration and perennial crops, and more splits in coarse-textured sandy and light soils with low CEC.
  - Submerged rice soil: ammoniacal and urea-type fertilizers are most suitable.
  - Acidic upland soils in the rainy season: ammoniacal fertilizers are most suitable (ammonium is adsorbed, reducing leaching). Highly acidic upland soils: urea is preferred to ammonium sulphate.
  - Alkaline upland soils of low rainfall regions: nitrate fertilizers preferred (ammonia lost by volatilization otherwise).
  - Lime one to two weeks before applying urea or ammoniacal fertilizer, since ammonia is lost if applied together with lime.
  - Almost 70% of N in urea broadcast on flooded soil is lost by volatilization, immobilization and denitrification.
  - Measures to reduce loss: urea super granules or briquettes (clayey soils with CEC above 10 cmol(+)/kg); sulphur or lac coated urea for intermittent flooding; mix urea with moist soil and keep 24 to 48 hours; mix urea with neem cake (5:1); deep placement or plough-sole placement; neem extract coating.
  - Where split application is not possible because of water stagnation, give the full N dose basal as neem coated or coal tar coated urea.
  - Foliar urea 5% where quick response is needed (up to 15% with power sprayers). Use fresh urea to avoid biuret toxicity.

- **Phosphorus management**
  - Acid soils should be amended with lime, dolomite or magnesium silicate before applying phosphatic fertilizers (reduces fixation).
  - Slightly acid, neutral or mildly alkaline soils: water-soluble phosphatic fertilizers. Strongly acidic soils: rock phosphate or citrate-soluble forms.
  - Perennial crops like rubber, oil palm, coffee, tea and cardamom: rock phosphate can be applied.
  - Where soil available P is medium to high, P application can be skipped; for low P soil apply 45 kg P2O5/ha.
  - Seasonal crops need P early: apply at seeding or planting. Top dressing P is wasteful. Excess phosphate may cause deficiency of zinc, boron and other micronutrients.
  - Black soil (Chittur taluk, Palakkad): single superphosphate (water-soluble P) is most suitable.

- **Potassium management**
  - Muriate of potash for most crops; use potassium sulphate for tobacco and potato (chloride injury).
